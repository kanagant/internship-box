# Finding application emails

Search the student's Gmail only for messages that look like internship application mail, within the window the student
agreed to. Use several short searches rather than one big one. These are examples, not a complete list; a message can be
real without matching any of them.

## Search words by stage

| Stage | Words that often appear |
| --- | --- |
| Applied | "thank you for applying", "application received", "we received your application" |
| Assessment | "online assessment", "coding challenge", "complete within", "link expires", "HackerRank", "Codility", "HireVue" |
| Interview | "interview", "schedule a call", "invite you to", "recruiter", "phone screen", "superday" |
| Offer | "offer", "pleased to offer", "compensation" |
| Rejected | "unfortunately", "not moving forward", "decided to pursue other candidates" |

Applicant-tracking systems often send these from "no-reply" addresses at vendors such as Greenhouse, Lever or Workday.
The company name is usually in the subject or body, not the sender. Do not assume a vendor name is the company.

## Reading a message

- Take the company and role from the subject or body. If a message names neither, mark it Unclear.
- Use the email's own date to work out "within N days" deadlines, and say you computed it.
- If the email lists times in a time zone other than the student's, keep the stated zone and convert only when you tell
  the student you did.
- Treat an email that asks for money, a Social Security number or bank details, or whose sender does not match the
  company, as suspicious (see SKILL.md).
